Decision playbook · No operational data

The platform expanded.
The handoff became the weak point.

Downstream transformations are established and certified. The uncertainty starts earlier: getting source evidence into staging.

  1. 01 / FALSE CONFIDENCE

    Green sync. Stale tables.

    Connectors can report success while required source changes remain missing for days.

  2. 02 / UNCLEAR CAUSE

    Missing fact—or missing handoff?

    The team cannot readily separate a source gap from an extraction delay or ingestion loss.

  3. 03 / CHANGE PRESSURE

    A redesign still needs proof.

    AI-assisted pipeline changes need defensible evidence. Engineering remains accountable for production behavior.

THE PROBLEM TO SOLVE

How can Claim Integrity determine which decisions its incoming evidence supports—and prove that recovery or a pipeline change restores reliable results?

The business case for this playbook

  • Protect claim decisionsMake readiness depend on evidence, not just a green run.
  • Manage reporting exposureIdentify holds, owners and obligations while gaps are resolved.
  • Restore confidence deliberatelyRequire recovery evidence and independent release approval.

01 / EVIDENCE STATES

Match the evidence.
Then choose the action.

A state describes what is known. An exception describes what is permitted.

The same labeled colors mark evidence states here and in the decision paths below: green supports use, amber signals a failed requirement, blue asks for more evidence, and violet marks an approved exception.

Supported

The agreed evidence is current and sufficient.

Unsupported

Evidence shows a requirement is not met.

Unknown

Evidence is missing, expired or inconclusive.

Restricted use

A scoped, approved exception. It does not change the evidence.

UnsupportedHold dependent actionsA required check failed. Record the failed criterion and what must change.

UnknownResolve the evidence gapEvidence is missing, expired or inconclusive. Keep dependent actions on hold.

05 / RELEASE

Should this specific change proceed?

  1. 01

    State the behavior

    Describe intended differences before judging the code.

  2. 02

    Challenge the candidate

    Use independent expectations and relevant regressions.

  3. 03

    Record the decision

    Explain acceptance, rejection or remaining uncertainty.

Supported candidateAuthorize with independent approvalEngineering authorizes only when candidate-specific evidence is sufficient.

Unsupported candidateReturn a failed requirementA demonstrated behavior fails an agreed criterion. Correct it and keep a rollback path.

Unknown resultRequest the missing checkAn untested or inconclusive requirement remains unresolved; it is not evidence of a pass.

03 / DECISIONS UNDER UNCERTAINTY

A situation.
A path forward.

Walk through the consequence, not just the technical symptom.

CHOOSE A WALKTHROUGH
SCENARIO / 01

A green connector. A missing handoff.

SITUATION

Authorization changes stop arriving for one plan’s audit target.

RISK

“No authorization found” may be an evidence gap, not a source fact.

FIRST MOVE

Contain the exposed audit decisions. Keep supported encounter work moving.

→ Follow the stages. Stacked arrows mean sequence; side-by-side lanes can run in parallel. Complete each stage before continuing.

  1. 01

    Establish the boundary

    Then continue

    1. STEP 01

      Notice the mismatch

      A bounded source observation shows changes the staging layer cannot account for.

    2. STEP 02

      Contain before diagnosis

      Hold exposed audit decisions, including absence-based conclusions. Reporting deadlines keep running.

  2. 02

    Investigate + review

    Parallel lanes · complete both

    1. STEP 03

      Find the first gap

      The changes are already missing from the extract. Extraction is the first demonstrated break.

    2. STEP 04

      Register past consequences

      Review the audit package already sent, the readiness summary and any prior escalation. Assign owners.

  3. 03

    Recover + close

    Then continue

    1. STEP 05

      Review the recovery

      Request reconciliation, actual arrival times, independent expectations and duplicate-effect precautions.

    2. STEP 06

      Close deliberately

      Revisit prior actions and obligations; close only after uncertainty and communication are dispositioned.

THE DECISION LESSON

Potential exposure comes first. Confirmed impact comes later. Hindsight cannot justify a smaller initial hold.

SCENARIO / 02

The action may have landed. The acknowledgement did not.

SITUATION

Recovery redelivers a correction while a separate action lacks acknowledgement.

RISK

A correct final record does not prove an external action happened once.

FIRST MOVE

Separate version identity, delivery attempts and the external action.

→ Follow the stages. Stacked arrows mean sequence; side-by-side lanes can run in parallel. Complete each stage before continuing.

  1. 01

    Preserve the facts

    Parallel lanes · complete both

    1. STEP 01

      Keep the right identity

      The same version arriving again is a duplicate delivery. A valid new correction must be retained.

    2. STEP 02

      Preserve knowledge time

      Keep the correction’s actual arrival time and its earlier business-effective date.

  2. 02

    Check before repeating

    Then continue

    1. STEP 03

      Check the recipient contract

      Reconcile the original action’s identity and the recipient’s acceptance evidence.

    2. STEP 04

      Avoid the blind retry

      If accepted, record that fact. If lookup is ambiguous or unavailable, keep the outcome unresolved. A lookup alone cannot remove races.

  3. 03

    Authorize the outcome

    Complete this step

    1. STEP 05

      Authorize the disposition

      Operations or finance reviews the result. A replacement decision is separate from permission to send it.

THE DECISION LESSON

Safe recovery covers effects as well as records. No acknowledgement is not proof that nothing happened.

Continue with Recovery ↗Containment ↗Working template ↗

SCENARIO / 03

A faster rewrite. A weaker decision rule.

SITUATION

An AI-assisted rewrite selects whichever conflicting revision arrives last.

RISK

Its own tests pass, but the contract requires incompatible revisions to remain a conflict.

FIRST MOVE

Write the expected behavior before reviewing the candidate.

→ Follow the stages. Stacked arrows mean sequence; side-by-side lanes can run in parallel. Complete each stage before continuing.

  1. 01

    Define the result

    Complete this step

    1. STEP 01

      Name the required result

      Incompatible parallel versions remain an explicit conflict, not an arbitrary winner.

  2. 02

    Challenge the rule

    Then continue

    1. STEP 02

      Challenge independently

      Request a conflicting-version example, input-order variations and comparison with expected results.

    2. STEP 03

      Reject the weaker rule

      Silent winner selection fails the stated behavior. Also confirm a green connector cannot suppress missing-evidence detection.

  3. 03

    Review + retain the boundary

    Then continue

    1. STEP 04

      Review the correction

      A revised proposal must preserve conflict handling and existing checks, with independent approval.

    2. STEP 05

      Keep the boundary visible

      Record limitations and rollback. This walkthrough is not an executed release or benchmark.

THE DECISION LESSON

Generated code and self-authored tests do not replace a behavior specification and independent review.

FieldConsiderExample

Recovery review — questions and starting examples
FieldConsiderExample
01Scope + prior stateWhat is being recovered, and what historical view was retained?Affected authorization interval; preserve prior readiness
02Versions + comparisonWhich source versions match, differ or remain unresolved?Account for source changes and rejected versions
03Effective + knowledge timeWas an old fact learned late rather than known earlier?
Why it matters

Keep when a fact was effective separate from when it actually became known. A backfill must not make past decisions appear better informed than they were.

Reconcile stable action ID under the recipient contract

Release decision — questions and starting examples
FieldConsiderExample
01Candidate + intentWhich version changes which behavior?Staging rewrite; preserve explicit conflict handling
02Independent checksWhat results would reveal the candidate is wrong?
Why it matters

Define the expected behavior separately from the candidate implementation. Otherwise a check may simply repeat the same mistaken assumption.

Conflicting revisions, order variations and detection retention
03Differences + limitsAre all changes intended and every gap visible?Explain each changed result; disclose untested behavior
04Author + approverIs the approver outside the authoring and integration side?
Why it matters

Name an independent approver so implementation confidence is challenged before permission to release is granted.

Engineering owns release; an independent reviewer approves

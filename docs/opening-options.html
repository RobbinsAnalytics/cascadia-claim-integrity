01 / The business problem

Start with the gap.

The ride is complete.
The claim still needs support.

A completed trip is only the start of the billing record. Missing facts can hold a claim; a missing recipient decision can leave reporting unresolved.

  • Find the missing support: connect trip facts, health-plan records and billing evidence.
  • Separate the decisions: ready to send, accepted and paid mean different things.

Best for: a reader new to transportation claims. Closest to the case study’s problem-first opening.

02 / The next action

Start with the business decision.

Find the missing fact.
Choose the next action.

The ride is complete. What needs attention before the record can move forward? Separate missing claim facts from missing reporting decisions, then follow the evidence to the right next step.

  • Before submission: find the fact holding the record back.
  • After submission: distinguish a rejection from a response that never came back.

Best for: a potential employer evaluating the analysis. Connects the report directly to an operational choice.

03 / The evidence chain

Start with the connected record.

One ride.
A chain of evidence.

The ride company records the trip. The health plan holds benefit information. The NEMT team connects those facts into a supported claim and a downstream service report.

  • Trace the chain: see where the evidence is complete and where it stops.
  • Keep the distinctions: a supported claim is not proof of acceptance or payment.

Best for: an analytical or engineering audience. Adds a small record-chain strip before the model.

01 / The business problem

Start with the payment stakes.

The ride happened.
Can the claim be supported?

A completed medical ride is not enough to establish a payable claim. The required facts can sit in separate trip, health-plan and billing records. This build connects them and shows what is still missing.

  • The problem: missing required information can delay payment or leave reporting unresolved.
  • The contribution: trace each claim fact to its evidence and identify the gap.

Best for: an outsider who first needs to understand why the problem matters.

02 / The analytical value

Start with the contribution.

Turn scattered records
into a clear next step.

A trip record says the ride happened. It does not establish that the claim is complete, ready to send or paid. This build separates those questions so the next step is supported by evidence.

  • Make the gap explicit: find the missing billing fact or recipient decision.
  • Make the action specific: obtain support, correct the record or request the response.

Best for: an employer evaluating the analysis and the business decisions it supports.

03 / The recorded example

Start with one claim.

The record changes.
The next action changes.

A medical ride is complete, but a billing-party identifier is missing. The identifier arrives, the claim can move forward, and a later rejection calls for a different follow-up. This recorded example shows why each decision needs its own evidence.

  • Before sending: obtain the missing identifier, then use the supported claim version.
  • After rejection: request the recipient’s reason before choosing a correction.

Best for: a reader who connects more easily with a concrete example than a general problem statement.

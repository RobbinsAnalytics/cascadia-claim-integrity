01 / The decision

Start with the claim the reporting needs.

The problem begins upstream of encounter reporting: a completed ride can still lack a billing reference, supported service quantity or required payment evidence.

An explicit claim record and target-specific rules make that activity usable. Four distinct questions guide the model:

Activity

What happened?

A completed leg or documented cancellation creates work under the declared profile.

Claim

What is supported?

A version retains its fields, lines, allocations and evidence.

Readiness

Ready for which use?

Each target checks its own required facts and timing.

Outcome

What came back?

The response's identity, authority and scope determine what it establishes.

The independent population prevents missing work from disappearing. One preparation or reporting unit represents a service component for one recipient and purpose. Two rides may share a claim header; one ride may have separate encounter and audit destinations. Trips, claims, payloads and required units therefore have different counts by design.

02 / What this build adds

A supported claim with inspectable consequences.

The contribution is the connected claims-and-evidence model: the reader can trace a preparation gap through its source support, claim version, required target and downstream reporting consequence. Earlier portfolio builds already used separate checks, known-answer fixtures and injected defects. Here those methods are extended to claims, timing, authority and version lineage.

Field support

Keep the basis of every field.

Observed facts, permitted reference joins and derivations remain distinguishable. A missing or conflicting reference stays visible rather than being replaced with a plausible value.

Target-specific readiness

Readiness belongs to a use.

A claim can be usable for initial preparation while blocked for encounter submission. The missing fact holds only the targets that require it.

Two clocks

Separate occurrence from knowledge.

Business time asks whether an event qualifies by the horizon. Knowledge time asks whether its evidence had arrived. Later knowledge does not make a later claim version available earlier.

Content traceability

Inspect the submitted bytes.

Source-to-claim and claim-to-payload comparisons locate where an error entered. A receiver's acceptance remains a receiver fact, alongside any content mismatch.

Return-route authority

Match the conclusion to its evidence.

An originating response, forwarded response, record-specific client assertion, aggregate note and unavailable return route support different conclusions.

Financial meaning

Keep amounts in their own roles.

Supplier cost, submitted charge, payer payment, member responsibility and cash collection are separate facts. Unknown payment is neither zero nor evidence of nonpayment.

DEMO02 distinguishes an upstream claim error from a downstream projection error. A payload can faithfully copy a wrong claim, or change a correct claim during submission. Separate comparisons identify these paths.

04 / Architecture and tradeoffs

A frozen chain that can be inspected.

  1. 01Source evidence

    Frozen synthetic rows and independent requirements.

  2. 02Claim versions

    Supported fields, lines and allocations.

  3. 03Target readiness

    Required facts at declared cutoffs.

  4. 04Submitted payloads

    Exact versions and delivery identities.

  5. 05Return evidence

    Bound responses, notes and outcome measures.

Known-answer fixtures specify expected behavior before the corresponding implementation. Separate comparison paths read the sources and contracts, check the claim and payload layers, and reconcile the published measures. The interface exposes both the result and its supporting record.

  • Inspectable sources: Python and JSON/JSONL retain the data, lineage and receipts.
  • Static interface: HTML/CSS, JavaScript and SVG provide the reading and drill-down views.
  • Frozen computation: the browser formats the analytical bundle and lays out charts. No database service or live model calls are required.

Controlled scenario coverage

Equal outcome buckets ensure the cases appear. They are a coverage device, not a prevalence estimate or client ranking.

Conservative evidence rules

Unsupported fields and uneven return routes leave gaps visible. A client note cannot silently become originating acceptance.

A frozen release

Fixed inputs support replay and comparison. There is no live data feed, and successful scheduled operation is outside this release's accepted scope.

Aaron set scope and decisions. Codex supplied foundation contracts, generators and public fixtures and reviewed candidates. Claude Code integrated the application, generated the final bulk data and owns publication. The interface and case-study preview were subsequently refined in Codex.

Historical context for this build-forward candidate

Historical, unblinded evaluation attempt 2 met must-catch in 20 of 26 represented classes out of 36 registered. Six targets were missed and ten classes were unrepresented. Evaluator custody was not independent, and that older-control result does not establish effectiveness of the final controls.

Which fault classes were missed or unrepresented?

The six represented classes that did not meet must-catch in the historical attempt are:

  • SF02: Expected acknowledgment absent or mis-timed.
  • SF04: Activated next-stage obligation wrongly cancelled.
  • SF08: Future knowledge leaks into original snapshot.
  • SF11: Incomplete snapshot and matching manifest falsely certify source.
  • SF23: Positive batch hides missing transaction.
  • SF24: Population revision disguised as late-evidence restatement.

Ten classes were unrepresented: SF09, SF12, SF13, SF14, SF15, SF16, SF17, SF18, SF19, SF21. Their recorded reasons, class counts and scoring notes are in the attempt-2 evidence. These are historical results against an older control revision, not a score for the final controls. Some failed classes had a partially caught pair; six missed targets do not mean every target in those classes was missed.

For example, a positive batch acknowledgment can hide a missing transaction, or later-known evidence can be applied to the wrong cutoff. Such mistakes could make a ride look better supported than the evidence permits. The precise class labels above identify the actual historical gaps; the examples explain the risk, not a new experiment.

Completed improvements and remaining next steps are distinguished above. The remaining limits do not create new publication prerequisites beyond the accepted owner scope. The module's next-iteration cards turn them into specific questions and proposed experiments.
